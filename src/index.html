Are Credit Spreads Blowing Out?

Credit is the confirmer. The yield curve hints a year or two ahead and housing rolls over quarters ahead; corporate spreads blow out when the event is underway, and sometimes when there is no event at all. This page keeps a century of score: the Moody's quality spread monthly since 1919, every blowout dated at its complacency trough and its alarm, financial conditions since 1971, and Canada's spread record drawn ending exactly where its sources ended. Nothing here is a forecast.

Across the … episodes attached to a recession, the complacency trough led it by a median of … months, but the alarm itself led in only … of them: nearly every time, spreads crossed the threshold after the recession had already begun. Compare the housing page, where the crests led, and the yield page, where the inversions led. The sequencing this family was built around, curve hints, housing rolls, credit confirms, is computed across the three tables, not asserted.

The false alarms are the other half of the story: … blowouts came with no recession inside the window, and nearly every one has a name traders remember: the 1966 credit crunch, Continental Illinois in 1984, the LTCM aftermath, the 2011 euro stress, the 2015-16 energy bust, the 2022 rate shock. Credit breaks more often than the economy does. The recent tape: option-adjusted spreads

High yield, investment grade, and the CCC tier that blows out first. Three years is all there is: ICE now licenses FRED to redistribute only a sliding window (verified against both the keyless and keyed endpoints), although the underlying indexes reach 1996. Shown because OAS is the number the market quotes; the deep history on this page rides the Moody's series above, which nobody has truncated.

Financial conditions, weekly since 1971

The Chicago Fed's NFCI: 105 indicators of risk, credit and leverage in one index. Zero is the 1971-onward average; positive is tighter, negative looser. The dashed adjusted variant removes what the business cycle alone would explain. The whole index is re-estimated weekly, so its history moves a little routinely; the revision log below records that honestly.

Canada: a record that ends, shown ending

Two real Canadian credit spreads, both closed. The corporate bond spread is the McLeod, Young and Weir 10-industrials average over the Government of Canada long-bond average, monthly 1948 until the corporate average stopped being compiled in December 1988. The funding spread is prime corporate paper over 3-month bills, 1962 until CDOR-era publishing ended in December 2018. Nothing keyless replaced either; live Canadian corporate spreads are paywalled index data (FTSE/TMX). The gap is drawn, not filled. Shading is the C.D. Howe chronology.

Sources and construction

United States

  • The century spine: Moody's seasoned Baa minus Aaa corporate yields, monthly since January 1919, computed here with both raw legs shipped. Daily continuation from DBAA minus DAAA (1986). Same publisher, same basis, drawn together and labelled.
  • Against Treasuries, as published: BAA10Y (daily since 1986) and AAA10Y (1983). Not recomputed.
  • The recent tape: ICE BofA OAS via FRED, high yield, investment grade, CCC and lower. Redistribution is limited to a sliding window of roughly three years (probed 2026-09-07, identical with and without an API key); the truncation is stated wherever these are drawn.
  • Conditions: Chicago Fed NFCI and ANFCI, weekly since 1971.
  • Recession bands: NBER dates via USREC, from the shared dataset, drawn peak month through trough.

Canada

  • All from Statistics Canada table 10-10-0122-01 (Bank of Canada financial market statistics, last Wednesday of the month), every vector resolved from cube metadata and fetched with its live English title verified: v122490 (MYW 10 industrials, 1948-1988, terminated), v122487 (GoC over-10-year average, 1936, live), v122491 (prime corporate paper 3 month, 1956-2018, terminated), v122531 (treasury bills 3 month, 1962, live).
  • Recession bands: C.D. Howe Institute Business Cycle Council chronology, from the shared dataset.

Plumbing

  • FRED is fetched keyless-first (public CSV), keyed API as fallback; StatCan WDS has no keys. A failed fetch carries the stored series forward and says so.
  • Refreshes daily via cron: Moody's dailies post next morning, NFCI lands Wednesdays, StatCan monthly on its own calendar. The stamp above says when data last moved.
  • Series follow the shared econ-core contract used across housing, yield, jobs, debt and diesel, with a vintages field reserved for as-published views.
  • Raw data: /api/data, or the files under /data/.
  • Assembled with Claude (Anthropic). Measured spreads, published indexes, and computed history with the rule printed; no forecasts, no fair-value calls.
